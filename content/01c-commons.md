## A shared resource we take care of together

Open research information is the metadata about our publications, datasets, software, people, organisations, projects and funding, spread across CRIS systems, repositories, OpenAlex, OpenAIRE and Crossref. No single institution owns it. Every institution contributes to it, and every institution depends on it: for open access monitoring, for strategic analyses, for research assessment. That makes it a **shared resource**, a commons.

> Naming the shared resource is the first step in organising a commons. A commons only stays healthy when the community that uses it also takes care of it: agreeing what good looks like, measuring it in the open, and each doing their part to improve it at the source.

That is what PID2Portal set in motion for open research information:

- **We defined the resource together.** The Dutch Barcelona Declaration signatories brought their use cases and agreed which parts matter most: DOIs, ROR and ORCID.
- **We made its state visible to all.** The ORI dashboard shows, institution by institution and in the open, where the shared resource is incomplete or inconsistent.
- **We turn individual fixes into collective care.** Interventions, scored on effort and impact and shared between signatories, help everyone improve the source, not just their own copy.
- **We keep the commons itself open.** The data lake and its pipelines are public and reproducible, on infrastructure the community controls.

For the Netherlands Research Portal, the DURF project now organises this as a research commons, with shared governance and funding. For open research information itself, that case still has to be made. PID2Portal lays the groundwork; together with the signatories, and in BROCCOLI, we want to take it further.
